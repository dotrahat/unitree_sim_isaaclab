# Arm tracking accuracy — G1-23 + BrainCo teleoperation

**Question:** *If my hand is at this x,y,z, does the robot's palm go to that x,y,z — and
what is the error between desired and achieved?*

Position (mm) and orientation (deg) are reported **separately** throughout.
Generated by `arm_tracking_analysis.py`; verified by `verify_metrics.py`.

---

## 1. The three-way decomposition

| | what it is | source |
|---|---|---|
| **DESIRED** | the operator's requested wrist pose | `actions.<side>_wrist_target_SE3` — the exact 4×4 handed to `solve_ik` |
| **IK-ACHIEVABLE** | best the 5-DoF arm's IK could do with that request | FK(`actions.<side>_arm.qpos`) = FK(`sol_q`) |
| **ACHIEVED** | where the palm actually ended up | FK(`states.<side>_arm.qpos`) |

- **TOTAL** = DESIRED → ACHIEVED — the headline: your hand vs the robot's palm
- **REACHABILITY** = DESIRED → IK-ACHIEVABLE — 5 DoF cannot represent a 6-DoF request
- **SERVO** = IK-ACHIEVABLE → ACHIEVED — the arm missing its own target

The DESIRED pose is already in the robot's waist frame in Unitree URDF convention, so it is
directly comparable to FK output with no transform. `G1_23_ArmIK` does **not** apply
`scale_arms` (commented out, `robot_arm_ik.py:564`), so there is no human→robot rescaling
to undo — your hand at (x,y,z) is requested at literally that (x,y,z).

### Reading it

**The arm goes almost exactly where it is told. It is not told where your hand is.**
The servo contributes ~6 mm and ~1°. The reachability term contributes ~34 mm and ~16°, and
it is what dominates the total. On a 5-DoF arm this is kinematics, not control — no amount
of gain tuning changes it.

Position and orientation degrade differently, which is why they must be reported separately:
the IK cost weights translation **100× more than rotation** (`50 *` translational vs
`0.5 *` rotation cost, `robot_arm_ik.py`), so the solver spends its two missing DoF buying
position accuracy and pays for it in wrist orientation.

### The reachability error is a real workspace limit, not a calibration offset

There **is** a constant offset in the IK residual (left −16.7, −17.0, +12.5 mm;
right −20.1, +12.7, +11.5 mm — magnitude ~27 mm, mirror-symmetric in y, consistent with the
hard-coded head→waist constants at `tv_wrapper.py:117-118`). But removing it barely moves
the median (21.3 → 20.9 mm on `episode_0025`). What actually drives the error is reach
distance (`metrics_ik_reach.csv`, Plot 5):

| requested pose distance from waist | median IK residual (left) | (right) |
|---|---|---|
| 358–411 mm | 13.6 mm | 10.8 mm |
| 411–451 mm | 10.9 mm | 19.0 mm |
| 451–560 mm | 34.7 mm | 28.7 mm |
| 560–750 mm | **61.6 mm** | **54.1 mm** |

A constant frame offset would be flat. This grows steeply with reach — genuine 5-DoF
workspace limitation.

### Per-session (the two target sessions differ, and that is informative)

| session | term | pos median (L/R) | ori median (L/R) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `episode_0025` (57 s) | total | 27.9 / 24.4 mm | 18.4 / 20.0° |
| | reachability | 21.3 / 22.3 mm | 18.0 / 18.6° |
| | servo | 4.6 / 4.6 mm | 1.0 / 1.3° |
| `episode_0026` (39 s) | total | 57.4 / 74.6 mm | 9.2 / 15.0° |
| | reachability | 54.6 / 66.3 mm | 9.3 / 14.5° |

`episode_0026` reached further out — much larger position infeasibility, *lower* orientation
error. `episode_0025` involved more wrist rotation — the reverse. The servo term is stable
at 4.6–8.4 mm across both. That contrast is worth showing: the 5-DoF penalty moves between
position and orientation depending on what the operator asks for.

## 3. Joint-space servo detail

`metrics_joint.csv`, `metrics_aggregate_joint.csv`. Physical, all usable sessions:
every joint below **1.0° MAE** and **3.8° p95**; measured lags commanded by **66.7 ms**
(2 samples) at 9 of 10 joints, cross-correlation **0.994–1.000**; compensating for that lag
drops per-joint error to **0.21–0.59°**. In steady state (commanded speed < 2 °/s sustained
0.5 s) **all ten joints settle within 0.13°–0.41°**.

## 5. Data inventory and integrity

`sessions.csv` lists all episodes with `included` and `excluded_reason`.

| group | episodes used | samples |
|---|---|---|
| physical, with wrist targets | `0025`, `0026` | 2 892 |
| physical, servo only | `0022` | 18 110 |
| sim, servo only | `0023`, `0024` | 153 |
| **excluded** | `0000`–`0021` (22 episodes) | 43 727 |

**Why 0000–0021 are excluded.** All of them are 5-DoF-per-arm G1-23 data — there is no
7-DoF arm data anywhere in this dataset — but the recorder stored the 10-element dual-arm
vector as two *overlapping* 7-element slices (`q[:7]` and `q[-7:]`), so the stored
`right_arm[0:4]` is a byte-for-byte copy of `left_arm[3:7]`, verified on 100 % of frames.
That is losslessly reconstructible, but no reported number should rest on a repair step.
Fixed in the recorder from `episode_0022` onward.

**Other flags.** Sim episodes repeat the measured vector on 33 % of frames (DDS state at
~half the 30 Hz record rate); physical shows 1 stuck frame in 18 110. No per-sample
timestamps exist, so 30 Hz is assumed (validated at 29.8–29.9 Hz against the sim's
1-second-resolution `sim_state._timestamp`). Episodes still being recorded are skipped as
incomplete and listed in `sessions.csv`. No sample is dropped within any included episode.

**End-to-end latency is not available** — no per-sample timestamps, and the only clock is
sim-only at 1-second resolution from a single process. The 66.7 ms above is
command-to-measurement inside one loop, not operator-to-robot.

## 7. Slide-ready

> We measured, per sample, where the operator's hand asked the robot's palm to be and where
> it actually went — 2 892 samples of physical teleoperation on the G1-23 with BrainCo hands.
> The total desired-to-achieved error is a median of **39.5 mm in position** and **16.0° in
> orientation**. Decomposing it shows the control system is not the problem: the arm tracks
> its own commanded target to **5.9 mm** and **1.2°**, while **~34 mm and ~16°** come from the
> 5-DoF arm being unable to represent a full 6-DoF wrist pose. That reachability error is a
> genuine workspace limit rather than a calibration offset — it grows from **13 mm** for
> nearby targets to **62 mm** at full reach, whereas a fixed offset would be constant.
> Practically: the robot goes where it is told to within about 6 mm, but on a 5-DoF arm it
> can only be told to go within about 34 mm of where your hand actually is.
