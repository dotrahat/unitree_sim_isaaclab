# Arm tracking accuracy — G1-23 + BrainCo teleoperation

**Question:** *If we tell the arm to move to a certain position, does it really go there,
at that exact position or not?*

Two groups: **simulation** and **physical robot**. Generated by `arm_tracking_analysis.py`
(this directory); all numbers come from its outputs `metrics.csv`, `metrics_palm.csv`,
`metrics_aggregate_joint.csv`, `metrics_aggregate_palm.csv`, `sessions.csv`, `metrics.json`.

---

## 1. Data inventory

### 1.1 What the recorder writes

Recording is done by `EpisodeWriter` (`xr_teleoperate/teleop/utils/episode_writer.py`;
identical copy at `unitree_sim_isaaclab/tools/episode_writer.py`), driven from
`teleop/teleop_hand_and_arm.py:799`.

**Layout:** `<task_dir>/<task_name>/episode_XXXX/` containing `data.json`, `colors/`,
`depths/`, `audios/`. One JSON per episode; images are JPEG side-cars.

**`data.json` schema:** `{info, text, data}` where `data` is a list of per-sample items:

| field | content | units |
|---|---|---|
| `idx` | monotonic sample counter | – |
| `states.<side>_arm.qpos` | **measured** joint position, read back over DDS | **radians** |
| `actions.<side>_arm.qpos` | **commanded** joint target = `sol_q`, the IK solution | **radians** |
| `states/actions.<side>_ee.qpos` | BrainCo hand joints (6) | normalised |
| `states/actions.body.qpos` | locomotion state/command — **empty in every episode here** | – |
| `qvel`, `torque` | **always empty lists** — never populated by the recorder | – |
| `sim_state` | sim only: reset state + `task_name` + `_timestamp` | – |
| `colors`, `depths` | relative image paths | – |

**Joint ordering — 5 DoF per arm.** The G1-23 arm is
`shoulder_pitch, shoulder_roll, shoulder_yaw, elbow, wrist_roll` (`G1_23_JointArmIndex`,
motor indices 15–19 and 22–26). There is no wrist pitch and no wrist yaw. The script asserts
this order against the pinocchio reduced-model order (`nq = 10`) at startup, so a
joint-order mismatch would fail loudly rather than produce plausible-looking numbers.

**Sample rate:** nominally 30 Hz (`args.frequency`, enforced by a `time.sleep` at the bottom
of the main loop). **There are no per-sample timestamps.** The only clock in the format is
`sim_state._timestamp` — sim only, **1-second resolution**; its first-to-last slope over the
long sim episodes gives 29.8–29.9 Hz, which is what validates 30 Hz as the sample spacing.
Physical episodes carry no clock at all.

### 1.2 Sessions used

One task directory exists: `/home/cvl/xr_teleoperate/teleop/utils/data/pick cube/`,
25 episodes, 54 000 samples. No other episode store, HDF5, or LeRobot dataset for this
system was found on this machine. (`/home/cvl/g1a-retarget` holds parquet datasets, but
those are motion-retargeting source/output data, not teleoperation recordings, and contain
no measured robot state.)

**Inclusion rule: only episodes whose dual-arm vector was stored natively as a correct
5 + 5 split.**

| group | episodes used | samples | duration |
|---|---|---|---|
| **physical robot** | `episode_0022` | **18 110** | 604 s (10 min) |
| **simulation** | `episode_0023`, `episode_0024` | **153** | 5.1 s |

**Excluded: 22 episodes, 43 727 samples** (0000–0021). Every one of them is also 5-DoF-per-arm
G1-23 data — there is no 7-DoF arm data anywhere in this dataset — but the recorder stored
the 10-element dual-arm vector as two overlapping 7-element slices, `q[:7]` and `q[-7:]`
(code written for the 7-DoF G1-29). Those windows overlap by four elements, so the stored
`right_arm[0:4]` is a byte-for-byte copy of `left_arm[3:7]`, verified on **100 % of frames in
every affected episode**. The underlying 10-vector is losslessly reconstructible and an
earlier revision of this script did reconstruct it, but no reported number should rest on a
repair step, so those episodes are excluded outright. They are listed in `sessions.csv` with
`included=False` and their sample counts, so the exclusion is auditable rather than silent.
The recorder was fixed from episode 0022 onward (`half = len(current_lr_arm_q) // 2`).

### 1.3 Remaining caveats on the retained data

1. **The simulation group is thin, and it is not a reaching motion.** 153 samples, 5.1 s.
   The largest commanded palm vertical excursion in the whole sim group is **2 mm**, and the
   left/right statistics agree to three decimals — the signature of a reset/idle pose, not
   teleoperation. Sim numbers below are valid as a **static-hold** datapoint and nothing more:
   they do not characterise the simulator under a real reach, and no lag estimate is possible
   from them (§2.A). **Recording one 20–30 s sim reaching episode and re-running the script
   would close this — no code change needed.**
2. **Stale measured state in sim.** 33 % of sim frames repeat the previous measured vector
   bit-for-bit (29 of 90 in episode_0023). The DDS state feedback arrives at roughly half the
   30 Hz record rate. The physical session shows **1 stuck frame in 18 110**. This is a
   sim-side telemetry problem, not a servo property, and a second reason not to read a lag
   figure off the sim data.
3. **The physical group is a single session.** Statistics are pooled over 18 110 samples but
   only one 10-minute recording, so between-session variability is not represented. For
   reference, the excluded older physical sessions ranged from 5.5 mm to 42.3 mm palm MAE —
   i.e. this session is at the good end of what has been observed, and a second clean session
   would be worth recording before treating these numbers as the system's steady state.
4. **No sample was dropped within a retained episode.** Every metric is over the full episode.
   The only sub-selection is the steady-state window (§2.A), reported with its own explicit
   `n` alongside the all-sample `n`.
5. Large maximum errors were checked and are **genuine transients during fast commanded
   swings, not ±360° wrap artefacts** — the commanded ranges span those values continuously,
   and they are 27 samples in 181 100 joint-samples for episode_0022.

## 2. Metrics

Sample rate assumed 30 Hz (Δt = 33.3 ms), validated as in §1.1. Alignment for all metrics is
**by sample index** — commanded and measured are written in the same iteration of the same
loop, so index *is* the alignment. No resampling was performed and none is possible without
timestamps.

noise-fitting artefacts, not measurements. No sim lag figure is reported; the values sit in
`metrics.csv` for completeness only.

### C. IK feasibility residual — **not available from recorded data**

The operator's requested wrist pose (`tele_data.left_wrist_pose` / `right_wrist_pose`,
produced by `televuer/tv_wrapper.py`) is passed directly into `arm_ik.solve_ik(...)` at
`teleop/teleop_hand_and_arm.py:648` and then discarded. Only the IK *output* (`sol_q`) is
recorded. It is not present in any other log stream — grepped across the whole
`xr_teleoperate/teleop/` tree, the only other references are a standalone example script that
prints it to a console log that is not persisted. It cannot be back-computed either:
FK(`sol_q`) gives the pose the IK *achieved*, and the residual is by definition the part that
was thrown away.

**This gap matters more than it would on a 7-DoF arm.** With only 5 DoF per arm the wrist
cannot achieve an arbitrary 6-DoF pose, so some fraction of the operator's requested poses
were certainly unreachable — and nothing on disk says how often, or by how much. Everything
reported above is *servo* error (commanded joint target vs measured joint position); it does
**not** include the error the IK introduced before the command was ever issued.

**To obtain this metric, the recorder must log the 4×4 requested wrist pose per arm per
sample** — one extra field, e.g. `actions.<side>_wrist_target_SE3`, added alongside the other
`actions` entries at `teleop_hand_and_arm.py:747`. The residual is then FK(`sol_q`) minus the
stored target, split into position (mm) and orientation (deg).

### D. Vertical tracking

Vertical shortfall = `commanded_z − achieved_z` in mm; positive means the palm sits **below**
its target. **Plot 1** (`plot1_palm_height.png`).

| group | arm | mean shortfall [mm] | max shortfall [mm] | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **physical** | left | **−1.9** | +32.4 | 18 110 |
| **physical** | right | **−0.3** | +33.7 | 18 110 |
| sim (static hold) | left | +4.4 | +6.2 | 153 |
| sim (static hold) | right | +4.2 | +5.9 | 153 |

The physical robot shows **no systematic vertical sag**. Over ten minutes in which the command
sweeps the palm through **544 mm of vertical range**, the mean shortfall is **−1.9 mm** — i.e.
fractionally *above* target on average — with a worst-case instantaneous shortfall of
+32.4 mm during fast transients. The zoomed panel of Plot 1 shows a 20 s window containing a
484 mm reach where the achieved trace visibly tracks the commanded trace, slightly behind it.

The sim group cannot speak to vertical tracking under motion: its commanded vertical range is
2 mm. The +4.2 to +4.4 mm figure is a static offset, not a sag measurement.

### E. End-to-end latency — **not available from recorded data**

No per-sample timestamps exist anywhere in the episode format. The only clock is
`sim_state._timestamp`, which is (a) sim-only, (b) quantised to **1 second**, and (c) written
by a single process, so it cannot measure a cross-process interval even at its own resolution.
Image file mtimes are written asynchronously by a background worker thread draining a queue
and do not correspond to capture time.

Per the rules, no number is given. The 66.7 ms figure in §2.A is a *command-to-measurement*
lag measured in samples of a single loop; it is **not** operator-wrist-to-robot-motion
latency, and it excludes the XR capture, retargeting, and DDS transport legs entirely.

**To obtain this metric:** log a per-sample monotonic timestamp at three points — XR frame
capture, IK solve, and DDS state receipt — and either run all three in one process against
`time.monotonic()`, or synchronise the robot and host clocks (PTP/chrony) and record both.
Without clock synchronisation across processes, no defensible number is possible.

## 4. Slide-ready

> Over ten minutes of physical teleoperation on the G1-23 with BrainCo hands (18 110 samples,
> 5 DoF per arm), the commanded palm pose and the pose the robot actually reached differ by a
> median of **4.3 mm**, mean **5.7 mm**, p95 **14.4 mm**, with a mean orientation error of
> **1.2–1.5°**. Most of that is latency rather than inaccuracy: the measured arm lags the
> command by **66.7 ms** at nine of ten joints, at a cross-correlation of **0.994–1.000**, and
> compensating for that lag cuts the per-joint error to **0.21–0.59°**. When the operator
> holds still, **all ten arm joints settle to within 0.13°–0.41° of their target** and the
> palm sits a median of **1.6 mm** from the commanded position — so the arm does arrive. There
> is no systematic vertical sag: across 544 mm of commanded vertical range the mean shortfall
> is **−1.9 mm**. Two limits I state up front: the operator's *requested* wrist pose is never
> logged, so on a 5-DoF arm the unreachable-pose error cannot be separated from servo error,
> and there are no per-sample timestamps, so true end-to-end operator-to-robot latency is not
> measurable from these recordings.
