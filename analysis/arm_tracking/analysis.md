# Arm tracking accuracy — G1-23 + BrainCo teleoperation

**Question:** *If we tell the arm to move to a certain position, does it really go there,
at that exact position or not?*

Two groups are reported: **simulation** and **physical robot**, both on the current system.
Generated by `arm_tracking_analysis.py` (this directory); all numbers come from its outputs
`metrics.csv`, `metrics_palm.csv`, `metrics_aggregate_joint.csv`,
`metrics_aggregate_palm.csv`, `sessions.csv`, `metrics.json`.

---

## 1. Data inventory

### 1.1 What the recorder writes

Recording is done by `EpisodeWriter` (`xr_teleoperate/teleop/utils/episode_writer.py`;
identical copy at `unitree_sim_isaaclab/tools/episode_writer.py`), driven from
`teleop/teleop_hand_and_arm.py:799`.

**Layout:** `<task_dir>/<task_name>/episode_XXXX/` containing `data.json`, `colors/`,
`depths/`, `audios/`. One JSON per episode; images are JPEG side-cars.

**`data.json` schema:** `{info, text, data}` where `data` is a list of per-sample items:

| field | content | units |
|---|---|---|
| `idx` | monotonic sample counter | – |
| `states.<side>_arm.qpos` | **measured** joint position, read back over DDS | **radians** |
| `actions.<side>_arm.qpos` | **commanded** joint target = `sol_q`, the IK solution | **radians** |
| `states/actions.<side>_ee.qpos` | BrainCo hand joints (6) | normalised |
| `states/actions.body.qpos` | locomotion state/command — **empty in every episode here** | – |
| `qvel`, `torque` | **always empty lists** — never populated by the recorder | – |
| `sim_state` | sim only: reset state + `task_name` + `_timestamp` | – |
| `colors`, `depths` | relative image paths | – |

**Joint ordering** (`G1_23_JointArmIndex`, motor indices 15–19 and 22–26):
`shoulder_pitch, shoulder_roll, shoulder_yaw, elbow, wrist_roll` per arm, left then right.
The script asserts this against the pinocchio reduced-model joint order, so the same
10-vector feeds both the joint statistics and the FK.

**Sample rate:** nominally 30 Hz (`args.frequency`, enforced by a `time.sleep` at the bottom
of the main loop). **There are no per-sample timestamps.** The only clock in the format is
`sim_state._timestamp`, present in sim episodes only, at **1-second resolution**; its
first-to-last slope over the long sim episodes gives 29.8–29.9 Hz, which is what validates
30 Hz as the sample spacing used throughout. Physical episodes carry no clock at all.

### 1.3 Data-integrity problems found — read before quoting any number

1. **The simulation group is thin, and it is not a reaching motion.** 153 samples, 5.1 s,
   across two episodes. The largest commanded palm vertical excursion in the whole sim group
   is **2 mm**, and the left/right statistics agree to three decimals — the signature of a
   reset/idle pose, not teleoperation. Sim numbers below are valid as a **static-hold**
   datapoint and nothing more. They do not characterise the simulator under a real reach,
   and no lag estimate is possible from them (see §2.A). **To fix this, record one 20–30 s
   sim reaching episode with the current asset and re-run the script — no code change needed.**
2. **Joint-slicing defect in episodes 0000–0021 (recoverable, and recovered).** The recorder
   split the 10-element dual-arm vector as `q[:7]` / `q[-7:]`, code written for the 7-DoF
   G1-29. On the 5-DoF-per-arm G1-23 those slices **overlap by four elements**: the stored
   `left_arm` is `q[0:7]` and the stored `right_arm` is `q[3:10]`, so neither field is a real
   arm and `right_arm[0:4]` is literally a copy of `left_arm[3:7]`. This affects **6 of the 7
   physical sessions**. The true 10-vector is recovered losslessly as
   `concat(left[0:7], right[4:7])`; the script verifies the expected overlap elementwise on
   every frame before accepting the reconstruction — **0 violations across all 54 000
   samples**. Fixed in the recorder from episode 0022 onward
   (`half = len(current_lr_arm_q) // 2`). Anyone reading these episodes with naive
   `states["right_arm"]` will get mostly left-arm data.
3. **Stale measured state in sim episodes.** In sim, 33–47 % of frames repeat the previous
   measured vector bit-for-bit (episode_0023: 29 of 90). The DDS state feedback arrives at
   roughly half the 30 Hz record rate. Physical episodes show **0 stuck frames** (1 in
   18 110 for episode_0022). This is a sim-side telemetry problem, not a servo property, and
   it is a second reason not to read a lag figure off the sim data.
4. **No sample was dropped or excluded anywhere in this analysis.** Every metric is over the
   full episode. The only sub-selection is the steady-state window (§2.A), reported with its
   own explicit `n` alongside the all-sample `n`.
5. Large maximum errors (up to 89° at wrist_roll) were checked and are **genuine transients
   during fast commanded swings, not ±360° wrap artefacts** — the commanded ranges span those
   values continuously, and they are 27 samples in 181 100 for episode_0022.

## 2. Metrics

Sample rate assumed 30 Hz (Δt = 33.3 ms) everywhere, validated as in §1.1. Alignment for all
metrics is **by sample index** — commanded and measured are written in the same iteration of
the same loop, so index *is* the alignment. No resampling was performed and none is possible
without timestamps.

**On hardware the error is mostly lag.** A uniform **100 ms** (3 samples) shift roughly halves

**Lag is not estimable for the current sim data.** The two sim episodes are a near-static
hold, so there is almost no signal to correlate: the per-joint `k` values come back as
0 ms, 633 ms, 1933 ms, and in two cases the best correlation is *negative* (r = −0.37,
−0.47). These are noise-fitting artefacts, not measurements, and no sim lag figure is
reported. They are in `metrics.csv` for completeness only.

### C. IK feasibility residual — **not available from recorded data**

The operator's requested wrist pose (`tele_data.left_wrist_pose` / `right_wrist_pose`,
produced by `televuer/tv_wrapper.py`) is passed directly into `arm_ik.solve_ik(...)` at
`teleop/teleop_hand_and_arm.py:648` and then discarded. Only the IK *output* (`sol_q`) is
recorded. It is not present in any other log stream — grepped across the whole
`xr_teleoperate/teleop/` tree, the only other references are a standalone example script that
prints it to a console log that is not persisted. It cannot be back-computed either:
FK(`sol_q`) gives the pose the IK *achieved*, and the residual is by definition the part that
was thrown away.

**To obtain this metric, the recorder must log the 4×4 requested wrist pose per arm per
sample** — one extra field, e.g. `actions.<side>_wrist_target_SE3`, added alongside the other
`actions` entries at `teleop_hand_and_arm.py:747`. With that stored, the residual is
FK(`sol_q`) minus the stored target, split into position (mm) and orientation (deg).

### D. Vertical tracking

Vertical shortfall = `commanded_z − achieved_z` in mm; positive means the palm sits **below**
its target. **Plot 1** (`plot1_palm_height.png`).

| group | arm | mean shortfall [mm] | max shortfall [mm] | n |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **physical** | left | **−3.7** | +74.4 | 41 479 |
| **physical** | right | **−0.8** | +72.9 | 41 479 |
| sim (static hold) | left | +4.4 | +6.2 | 153 |
| sim (static hold) | right | +4.2 | +5.9 | 153 |

The physical robot shows **no systematic vertical sag**: the mean shortfall is slightly
*negative* on both arms, i.e. the palm sits fractionally above its target on average.
In episode_0022 the command sweeps the palm through **544 mm of vertical range** and the mean
shortfall over the full 10 minutes is **−1.9 mm**, max **+32.4 mm**. The zoomed panel of
Plot 1 shows a 20 s window containing a 484 mm reach where the achieved trace tracks the
commanded trace visibly but only slightly behind it.

The sim group cannot speak to vertical tracking under motion: its commanded vertical range is
2 mm. The +4.2 to +4.4 mm figure is a static offset, not a sag measurement.

### E. End-to-end latency — **not available from recorded data**

No per-sample timestamps exist anywhere in the episode format. The only clock is
`sim_state._timestamp`, which is (a) sim-only, (b) quantised to **1 second**, and (c) written
by a single process, so it cannot measure a cross-process interval even at its own
resolution. Image file mtimes are written asynchronously by a background worker thread
draining a queue and do not correspond to capture time.

Per the rules, no number is given. The 100 ms figure in §2.A is a *command-to-measurement*
lag measured in samples of a single loop; it is **not** operator-wrist-to-robot-motion
latency, and it excludes the XR capture, retargeting, and DDS transport legs entirely.

**To obtain this metric:** log a per-sample monotonic timestamp at three points — XR frame
capture, IK solve, and DDS state receipt — and either run all three in one process against
`time.monotonic()`, or synchronise the robot and host clocks (PTP/chrony) and record both.
Without clock synchronisation across processes, no defensible number is possible.

## 4. Slide-ready

> Across 41 479 samples of physical teleoperation (23 minutes, G1-23 + BrainCo), the
> commanded palm pose and the pose the robot actually reached differ by a median of
> **5.1 mm**, mean **8.8 mm**, p95 **27.3 mm**, with a mean orientation error of **2.0–2.5°**;
> in the most recent 10-minute session the median is **4.3 mm** over 544 mm of commanded
> vertical range. Most of that error is latency, not inaccuracy: shifting the command by
> **100 ms** roughly halves the per-joint error at every joint, at a cross-correlation of
> **0.997–0.999**. When the command stops moving, **nine of the ten arm joints settle to
> within 0.2–0.9° of their target** — so the arm does arrive; the one exception is the left
> shoulder-roll joint, which holds a persistent **1.3°** offset and is the largest remaining
> systematic error. There is no systematic vertical sag on hardware: the mean vertical
> shortfall is **−1.9 mm** over ten minutes of reaching. Two limits I will state up front:

> operator-to-robot latency is not measurable from these recordings.
